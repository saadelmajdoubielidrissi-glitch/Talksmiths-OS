📖 Personal Pronouns — The Complete Family

In English, we use pronouns instead of repeating people's names. Just like in French: je, tu, il, elle...

I → أنا → Je
The person speaking (yourself)
"I am a painter." — "I love football."
🇲🇦 Ana / 🇫🇷 Je
You → أنت/أنتِ → Tu / Vous
The person you're talking to (singular AND plural!)
"You are my son." — "You are all welcome."
🇲🇦 Nta / Nti / Ntouma / 🇫🇷 Tu / Vous
He → هو → Il
A man or boy you're talking about
"He is my son. He studies engineering."
🇲🇦 Huwa / 🇫🇷 Il
She → هي → Elle
A woman or girl you're talking about
"She is the headmistress. She works very hard."
🇲🇦 Hiya / 🇫🇷 Elle
It → (no equivalent) → Il / Elle / Ça
Things, animals, ideas (not people!)
"It is a beautiful painting." — "It is raining."
🇲🇦 No exact match — hadshi/hadak / 🇫🇷 Il (impersonnel), Ça
We → حنا → Nous
You + one or more other people
"We watch football together." — "We are a family."
🇲🇦 7na / 🇫🇷 Nous / On
They → هم → Ils / Elles
Other people (2 or more, men, women, or mixed)
"They are good players." — "They live in Kenitra."
🇲🇦 Huma / 🇫🇷 Ils / Elles
💡 English Bonus!
English is SIMPLER than French & Darija here:
Only "you" for nta, nti, ntouma, tu, AND vous! One word does it all! 🎉
🇲🇦 Sahl bezzaf! / 🇫🇷 Plus simple que le français!

Step 2 — 📖 Mini-Story (Reading)
A Family Portrait
10 min
Objective: Read a short text and identify all the pronouns. Understand who each pronoun refers to.
📋 Teacher Note: Read the story aloud first while Baba follows with his eyes. Then ask him to read it aloud. Circle or underline every pronoun together. Ask "Who is HE here? Who is SHE?" for each one. This builds the pronoun-referent connection that Darija speakers sometimes skip.

I am Fattah. I am from Morocco. I live in Mehdya with my family.

My wife is Lamia. She is a headmistress. She is very kind and hardworking. We have two sons.

My first son is Saad. He lives in Hungary. He is a language teacher. My second son is Anas. He is an engineering student. He studies at ENSA in Kenitra.

We all love football. We watch Morocco games together. When Saad is not home, we call each other. We talk about the match, the players, and the tactics. It is our tradition.

They are my family. I am very proud of them.

📝 Comprehension — Answer in English

1. Where does Fattah live?

2. Who is Lamia? What does she do?

3. How many sons does Fattah have? What are their names?

4. What is the family tradition?

Step 3 — 🏗️ Grammar in Action
Pronouns + "To Be" = Magic Formula
10 min
Objective: Master the correct form of "to be" (am/is/are) with each pronoun.
📋 Teacher Note: This is THE foundational formula. Drill it like a football chant — repetition is key. Have Baba say each one while pointing (I AM → points to self, YOU ARE → points to you). Make it physical and rhythmic. Then cover the right column and quiz him.
📐 The Rule — Pronouns + "To Be"
I → am
Je suis
Ana ——
You → are
Tu es
Nta/Nti ——
He/She/It → is
Il/Elle est
Huwa/Hiya ——
We → are
Nous sommes
7na ——
They → are
Ils/Elles sont
Huma ——
🇫🇷 French Parallel: In French, "être" has 6 different forms (suis, es, est, sommes, êtes, sont). In English, "to be" has only 3 forms: am, is, are. English is actually easier here! 🎉
🇲🇦 F darija, ma kaynsh "to be" — "Ana Fattah" is enough! But in English you NEED it: "I am Fattah."

Step 5 — 🗣️ Speaking Challenge
Describe Your Family — In English!
10 min
Objective: Talk about your family for 2 minutes using all the pronouns you've learned.
📋 Teacher Note: This is the production moment. Set a timer for 2 minutes. Let him speak freely. Note errors but don't interrupt. After he finishes, give 3 compliments and 2 corrections.
